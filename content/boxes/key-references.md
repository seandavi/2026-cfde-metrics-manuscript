::: {.callout-note title="Key references"}

1. Wilkinson MD et al. The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship. *Scientific Data*, 2016 [@doi:10.1038/sdata.2016.18]. The first formal publication of the FAIR principles, which stress machines' ability to find and use data as well as reuse by people. They anchor the data quality and reusability domain, and by allowing authenticated access where necessary they keep FAIR distinct from openness.

2. Wilkinson MD et al. A design framework and exemplar metrics for FAIRness. *Scientific Data*, 2018 [@doi:10.1038/sdata.2018.118]. Operationalizes FAIR as 14 exemplar universal metrics covering each FAIR sub-principle, measurable by a semi-automated process, plus a template for community-specific metrics. This laid the groundwork for automated FAIR-compliance monitoring at the portfolio scale we describe, where metadata volume prevents manual audit.

3. Emissah H et al. Bibliometric analysis of neuroscience publications quantifies the impact of data sharing. *Bioinformatics*, 2023 [@doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btad746]. Provides the bibliometric evidence that fewer than 30% of secondary analyses formally cite source data, the core empirical basis for our argument that citation-count KPIs systematically understate reuse. It also finds reuse of shared NeuroMorpho.Org data constant for at least 16 years.

4. The GTEx Consortium. The Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project. *Nature Genetics*, 2013 [@doi:10.1038/ng.2653]. Describes the GTEx resource database and tissue bank for studying the relationship between genetic variation and gene expression in human tissues. The paradigmatic example of a high-reuse public biomedical data resource (8,630 platform citations as of January 2026), anchoring the public value domain and our platform-versus-content citation distinction.

5. El Burai Felix S et al. A Standard Framework for Evaluating Large Health Care Data and Related Resources. *MMWR Supplements*, 2024 [@doi:10.15585/mmwr.su7303a1]. A standard framework of evaluation constructs, questions, and metrics for large health care data and related resources, including which scientific questions each resource suits. Its representativeness and linkability criteria are particularly load-bearing for federated ecosystems like the CFDE, whose mission is cross-program query.

:::
